# Learning Lab Repository Instructions

## Canonical terminology gate

Every entry promoted into `concepts/` or `GLOSSARY.md` must use a modern,
established professional term. Do not coin a term, elevate a repository-local
phrase, or turn an explanatory paraphrase into a canonical dictionary entry.

A candidate term is admissible only when all of the following are true:

- the exact English term is used with substantially the same meaning by the
  relevant contemporary professional or academic community;
- its usage is corroborated by at least two independent authoritative sources,
  such as a current standard or specification, a peer-reviewed paper or
  established textbook, or documentation from independent mature projects or
  professional organizations;
- it names a transferable concept rather than one repository's class, field,
  workflow step, UI label, or local design shorthand; and
- the repository definition preserves the established meaning instead of
  silently narrowing, broadening, or repurposing it.

Search-result frequency, a single vendor's wording, an AI-generated phrase, or
the fact that a phrase sounds technically plausible is not sufficient
terminology evidence. When the evidence is ambiguous, fail closed: keep the
idea as descriptive prose in a lesson, learning record, or case lab and do not
add it to `concepts/` or `GLOSSARY.md`.

Existing entries are not grandfathered as proof of validity. Before modifying,
linking, or teaching an existing canonical entry as a professional term,
recheck it against this gate and rename, demote, or remove it if it does not
qualify. Repository validation can enforce structure and provenance fields but
cannot by itself prove community adoption; source review remains required.
