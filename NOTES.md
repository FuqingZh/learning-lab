# Teaching Notes

- Preferred language: Chinese.
- Preferred depth: build toward depth through very small lesson units; do not
  deliver exhaustive coverage in one response. Bottom-up explanations from
  biology, bytes, operating systems, databases, and distributed systems remain
  welcome when split across separate teach-check loops.
- Keep established technical terms in their standard English form instead of
  inventing or foregrounding Chinese translations; explain their meaning in
  Chinese when first needed.
- Teach one bounded capability per lesson. A bounded capability may require a
  small connected set of concepts; do not fragment a coherent framework into
  one-term turns. Define the lesson win and stopping condition before choosing
  its size.
- Present the coherent teaching block first, answer direct learner questions
  when they arise, and use one appropriately routed mastery check at the lesson
  stopping condition. Do not turn every explanation into an extended Socratic
  exchange or a sequence of fill-in-the-blank checks.
- Before teaching mechanisms within a new domain, establish its compact
  knowledge dependency map and show which foundational discipline owns each
  concept. Do not substitute a product workflow diagram for that map. Bounded
  lessons must be map-first, not detail-first.
- Preferred structure: define the layer and the problem before introducing the
  abstraction or command that solves it.
- Use concrete repository examples, counterexamples, small tables, and causal
  diagrams. Explicitly separate scientific identity from operational identity.
- Teach the repository-independent concept before its anchor-repository
  realization. Product table names, column positions, class names, paths,
  version numbers, and CLI spellings are case evidence rather than mastery
  targets unless the learner is explicitly studying that public contract.
- For computing topics, use a small implementation-neutral model first, then a
  minimal executable example, then the repository case, and finally a transfer
  question in a different system.
- Use a spiral curriculum: establish the whole-system map and the role of each
  layer before drilling into mechanisms. At the start of every lesson, state
  where the concept sits on the map, which adjacent layers it depends on, and
  whether the lesson is orientation, foundation, mechanism, case, or transfer.
- For an orientation or design lesson, check mastery with one explain-back that
  reconstructs the framework, applies it to a fresh case, and names a boundary.
  Do not quiz each node independently during the first framework pass.
- A sequence of locally valid micro-lessons is still off course if the learner
  cannot see the global architecture. Pause detail work and restore the map
  whenever this happens.
- Treat Bio Plot Platform and other repositories as case labs after the general
  and modern systems model is understood; never let repository routes, classes,
  or current implementation order become the curriculum spine.
- End bounded lessons with one unaided mastery check matched to the lesson
  target. On a material miss, repair the first blocking gap in a different
  representation and offer one focused retry.
- Do not add glossary entries merely because a term was presented or one local
  prompt was answered; wait for demonstrated understanding at the lesson's
  stated stopping condition.
- Keep track-specific review cues, anchor repositories, and progression in the
  owning track rather than this global file.
